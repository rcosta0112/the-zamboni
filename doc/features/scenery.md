# The scenery while the ship flies

> **[Agent note]** What exists (0.0.2, 2026-10-23). The plan and its history: [flying scene, pass 4](../plans/features/flying-scene.md). The decision: [decision log](../decision-log.md), *The scenery: a treadmill that looks like the owner's fly-by*. Code: `app/prototypes/0.0.2/src/terrain.ts`.

## What the player sees

A scene that flies (`"travel": { "terrain": "ice-plain" }` in its file: today `chapter-1`) shows the icy plain going by under the ship, through the cockpit windows and the viewport: white ice with a faint sheen, scattered rubble with long, sharp shadows, the haze at the horizon. The landed ground is hidden meanwhile and comes back when a landed scene starts.

The look is the owner's fly-by (`resources/models/The Zamboni 1.10 - fly by animation.blend`), its numbers ×1.486 (the ship was smaller then). The lighting is the game's.

## How it works

- **A treadmill:** the ship never moves. One group at the ship's centre holds the ground (a plane) and the rubble; the rubble slides back at the ship's speed and a piece that passes the rear edge comes back at the front somewhere new. Nothing is created while it runs.
- **A terrain is a data file** (`public/terrain/<id>.json`) and its meshes (`<id>.glb`). The icy plain: speed 120 m/s, the ship's belly 110 m up (owner, 2026-10-23, later; before that 174 m/s and 42 m, three times the fly-by's 14 m); the ice (the fly-by's light blue, linear (0.588, 0.741, 1), metalness 0.384, no glow, its roughness; white with a blue glow until the outside got its own light); the rubble (ten pieces of the fly-by's `Rubble`, 50 copies, scaled evenly 0–7.5, tipped up to 1 rad, centred on the ice then sunk by 20% of their height, the tallest about 47 m above the ice (owner, 2026-10-23, later: 50 rocks at 5 times the size; before that about 1,200 at 0–1.5, three times the fly-by's size, 30% fewer, lower, for fewer shadows under them), linear colour (0.8, 0.9, 1), roughness 1); **no clouds** (owner, 2026-10-23: removed, a new solution later; the code stays for any terrain: 3 cumulus made in code, smooth-shaded, 1.2–2.8 km across, their bases 200 m up, white, roughness 1, **10% opaque** (no depth written: where puffs overlap they add up), in a band 3.2 km each way; **they fade out with distance from the ship**, whole to 1.2 km, gone at 2 km, their shadows with them, so a cloud coming back at the front never pops in); the haze from 500 m, full at 3,000 m (owner, 2026-10-23, later; before that 800 m and 1,800 m). The cameras see to 3.6 km while flying. A backdrop (far shapes moving slower) and placeholder meshes made in code (`boulder`, `peak`) are supported, unused by the icy plain.
- **The outside's own light** ([plan](../plans/features/outside-light.md)): every material here (the ice, its shadows, the rocks) is lit by the game's sun (the same light: one direction inside and out, and its shadow map) and by **its own sky light**, Blender's world (the gradient's light from above, the cyan from below), kept out of the scene, so **the interior's dimming (`worldIndoors`) never reaches it** and nothing else gets it. The sun is tinted and scaled on these materials only, through their received shadow (three multiplies the sun's light by it), so the ship's shadow stays. Defaults: the sky at Blender's strength 1; **the sun at 0.65** (the fly-by's sun is at 13°: sin 0.22 on flat ice; ours at 20°: 0.34; the direction stays shared); **the sky's reflection on the ice 0.5**. With the fly-by's ice colour and no glow, the shadows are a saturated blue the ice shows through, and the ice goes from white toward the sun (the sun's glare) to blue away from it.
- **Seen through the windows** (owner, 2026-10-23: from inside a bit darker, just enough to notice the glass; the glass nearly invisible, as in Blender, its highlights kept): the ship's glass (see-through, metallic, untextured materials; `cutaway.ts`) adds its highlights (the cabin lights' reflections) whole and dims what's behind it by *glass: dims the view* (0.2: about 5% darker on screen) instead of laying its body over the view at its opacity (until then: 9% darker, the highlights at 30%). The panel's *Look* › *glass: dims the view*, *glass: highlights* (0.5).
- **The rubble's shadows** are its meshes again, flattened onto the ice along the sun in the shader: every point projected along the sun onto the ice (the buried ones too, up toward the sun: one flat projection, so each triangle's height carries across it exactly) and the pixels from below the ice left out (`maskNode`). Until 2026-10-23 the buried parts stayed where they were and drew their footprints as shadow (the owner: wrong shapes, shadows with no rock); drawn with the ice's material wholly in the sun's shadow: they look like the ship's own shadow on the ice. Sharp at any distance; no shadow map. **The clouds' shadows** are made the same way, as see-through as their clouds (10%).
- **The ice's roughness** is the fly-by's: two noises (cells about 119 × 475 m and 1.2 × 4.8 m, stretched along the flight) added and ramped from 0.452 to 1. The noise is a 256 × 256 repeating tile made at load, read at both scales; it moves with the ground.
- **Turning:** `flightHeading` turns the group (the world round the ship); the sun stays where it is. Used next by cutscene 2's exterior shot.
- **Cost:** 27 draws. Measured 2026-10-23 from outside, the frame rate uncapped (`flight-perf-probe.js`): about 3 ms a frame in all: moving the copies (1,700 then) 1 ms (CPU), the clouds 0.9 (the first 14 small ones; the 3 big see-through ones since: too little to measure), the rubble 0.8, the ice 0.6; nothing measurable for the roughness or the far plane. **The heap holds steady** over a minute (its low points 83 → 88 MB). `chapter-1` costs about 4 ms more than `landed` with the scenery hidden: that's not the scenery. Building a terrain stalls the first frames (new shaders): hidden under the start screen on a new game.

## Tuning

The tuning panel: *Look* › *flight: speed*, *height*, *heading*; *Flight: ground material* › colour, metalness, glow, the roughness's two ends and its two noises' sizes, and *print for the terrain file* (logs the values as the terrain file writes them); *Flight: outside light* › the sky's strength and its light from above and below, the sun's tint and strength (a share), the sky's reflections on the ice; *print the values*; *Flight: rocks* › *count* (all the scatter's copies, up to 6,000 without the meshes made again) and *size* (times the file's; their sinking follows), live, not printed (owner, 2026-10-23). (A *Flight: clouds* folder existed briefly, 2026-10-23: removed with the clouds.) Each flying scene's start loads the file's values.

## Not there yet

- Under a cloud's shadow, the rubble and the ship stay in the sun (only the ice is darkened; faint at 10%).
- Moving the copies could cost almost nothing (each pool moved whole, a copy rewritten only when it wraps): 1 ms back, if it's needed.
- The fly-by's gently bumped ground (ours is flat: the shadows need it) and its lower sun (13° against 20°).
- The ship's own shadow is about 40 m off at this height: outside the shadow map while she's inside.
- The thrusters' flames (notes for cutscene 1 in the plan).
