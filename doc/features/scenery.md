# The scenery while the ship flies

> **[Agent note]** What exists (0.0.2, 2026-10-23). The plan and its history: [flying scene, pass 4](../plans/features/flying-scene.md). The decision: [decision log](../decision-log.md), *The scenery: a treadmill that looks like the owner's fly-by*. Code: `app/prototypes/0.0.2/src/terrain.ts`.

## What the player sees

A scene that flies (`"travel": { "terrain": "ice-plain" }` in its file: today `chapter-1`) shows the icy plain going by under the ship, through the cockpit windows and the viewport: white ice with a faint sheen, scattered rubble with long, sharp shadows, the haze at the horizon. The landed ground is hidden meanwhile and comes back when a landed scene starts.

The look is the owner's fly-by (`resources/models/The Zamboni 1.10 - fly by animation.blend`), its numbers ×1.486 (the ship was smaller then). The lighting is the game's.

## How it works

- **A treadmill:** the ship never moves. One group at the ship's centre holds the ground (a plane) and the rubble, sliding back at the ship's speed.
- **The rubble round the camera** ([plan](../plans/features/scenery-around-camera.md); owner, 2026-10-23): the plain is a grid of cells (`cell`, 400 m), each cell's rocks decided by a hash of its place (how many from `density`, where, which shape, size, turn, tilts), so a cell always holds the same rocks. Each frame the rocks within `reach` (3.4 km along the ground, past the full haze) of **the camera being drawn** are shown: the game's, a watched conversation's or a cutscene's. Every camera sees the same rocks in the same places, a cut keeps them, the edge is always behind the haze, nothing repeats and nothing is stored. Long flights: the cells are worked out in doubles on the CPU, what's written stays near the group. `clear` (a backdrop's lane) is measured from the ship's line. The ground plane stays on the ship (7.2 km across: a camera within about 500 m of it is covered).
- **A terrain is a data file** (`public/terrain/<id>.json`) and its meshes (`<id>.glb`). The icy plain: **speed 360 m/s** (owner, 2026-10-23, last: 3 times, in every scene; 120 before), the ship's belly 110 m up (owner, 2026-10-23, later; before that 174 m/s and 42 m, three times the fly-by's 14 m); the ice (the fly-by's light blue, linear (0.588, 0.741, 1), metalness 0.384, no glow, its roughness; white with a blue glow until the outside got its own light); the rubble (ten pieces of the fly-by's `Rubble`, **1.32 per 10⁵ m²** (the owner's 50 in the band of the time, 2.1 × 1.8 km): **about 480 round the camera**, scaled evenly 0–7.5, tipped up to 1 rad, centred on the ice then sunk by 20% of their height, the tallest about 47 m above the ice (owner, 2026-10-23, later: 50 rocks at 5 times the size; before that about 1,200 at 0–1.5, three times the fly-by's size, 30% fewer, lower, for fewer shadows under them), linear colour (0.8, 0.9, 1), roughness 1); **no clouds** (owner, 2026-10-23: removed, a new solution later; the code stays for any terrain: 3 cumulus made in code, smooth-shaded, 1.2–2.8 km across, their bases 200 m up, white, roughness 1, **10% opaque** (no depth written: where puffs overlap they add up), in a band 3.2 km each way; **they fade out with distance from the ship**, whole to 1.2 km, gone at 2 km, their shadows with them, so a cloud coming back at the front never pops in); the haze from 500 m, full at 3,000 m (owner, 2026-10-23, later; before that 800 m and 1,800 m). The cameras see to 3.6 km while flying. A backdrop (far shapes moving slower) and placeholder meshes made in code (`boulder`, `peak`) are supported, unused by the icy plain.
- **The outside's own light** ([plan](../plans/features/outside-light.md)): every material here (the ice, its shadows, the rocks) is lit by the game's sun (the same light: one direction inside and out, and its shadow map) and by **its own sky light**, Blender's world (the gradient's light from above, the cyan from below), kept out of the scene, so **the interior's dimming (`worldIndoors`) never reaches it** and nothing else gets it. The sun is tinted and scaled on these materials only, through their received shadow (three multiplies the sun's light by it), so the ship's shadow stays. Defaults: the sky at Blender's strength 1; **the sun at 0.65** (the fly-by's sun is at 13°: sin 0.22 on flat ice; ours at 20°: 0.34; the direction stays shared); **the sky's reflection on the ice 0.5**. With the fly-by's ice colour and no glow, the shadows are a saturated blue the ice shows through, and the ice goes from white toward the sun (the sun's glare) to blue away from it.
- **Seen through the windows** (owner, 2026-10-23: from inside a bit darker, just enough to notice the glass; the glass nearly invisible, as in Blender, its highlights kept): the ship's glass (see-through, metallic, untextured materials; `cutaway.ts`) adds its highlights (the cabin lights' reflections) whole and dims what's behind it by *glass: dims the view* (0.2: about 5% darker on screen) instead of laying its body over the view at its opacity (until then: 9% darker, the highlights at 30%). The panel's *Look* › *glass: dims the view*, *glass: highlights* (0.5).
- **The roughness's fine noise at a quarter** (`groundFineAmount` 0.25, panel *Flight: ground material*; owner, 2026-10-23: *"the roughness is too strong"*): Blender's samples average the fly-by's fine noise (cells of about 1 m) out, the game drew it as a grain. Measured at frame 240 as the mean difference between the image and a slight blur of it: Blender's F12 0.22; the game at full 0.50, at half 0.31, at a quarter 0.24, without it 0.19.
- **Hills and valleys** (owner, 2026-10-23: like the fly-by's displaced ground, in every flying scene): the terrain's `hills` (`cell`: a grid's spacing across and along the flight, m; `height`: its range, m) give each grid point a random height, the ground's triangles between them, smooth-shaded (normals from the heights a cell either way, in the shader). The ground's points are the grid's: they move on with the ground into a cell, then back a whole cell onto the same heights, so the triangles never slide over the hills. The rocks sit on the hills (`Treadmill.heightAt`, the same triangles on the CPU). **The ice plain uses the fly-by's viewport values** (its subdivision level 4): points 37 x 74 m apart, 12.3 m of range. Its F12 render's (level 2: 149 x 297 m, 7.4 m) were tried first and changed almost nothing. The frame time with them is within the measurement's noise.
- **The rubble's shadows** are its meshes again, flattened onto the ice along the sun in the shader: every point projected along the sun onto the plane at the hills' height under it, then set on the hills where it lands (the buried ones too, up toward the sun), lit as the hills face there and the pixels from below the ice left out (`maskNode`). Until 2026-10-23 the buried parts stayed where they were and drew their footprints as shadow (the owner: wrong shapes, shadows with no rock); drawn with the ice's material wholly in the sun's shadow: they look like the ship's own shadow on the ice. Sharp at any distance; no shadow map. **The clouds' shadows** are made the same way, as see-through as their clouds (10%).
- **The ice's roughness** is the fly-by's: two noises (cells about 119 × 475 m and 1.2 × 4.8 m, stretched along the flight) added and ramped from 0.452 to 1. The noise is a 256 × 256 repeating tile made at load, read at both scales; it moves with the ground.
- **Turning:** `flightHeading` turns the group (the world round the ship); the sun stays where it is. Used next by cutscene 2's exterior shot.
- **Cost:** 27 draws. **Since the cells (2026-10-23):** making the copies 0.2 ms a frame (CPU, 476 rocks); from outside about 1.5 ms a frame more than with the old band's 50 rocks, within the measurement's spread. Before, measured 2026-10-23 from outside, the frame rate uncapped (`flight-perf-probe.js`): about 3 ms a frame in all: moving the copies (1,700 then) 1 ms (CPU), the clouds 0.9 (the first 14 small ones; the 3 big see-through ones since: too little to measure), the rubble 0.8, the ice 0.6; nothing measurable for the roughness or the far plane. **The heap holds steady** over a minute (its low points 83 → 88 MB). `chapter-1` costs about 4 ms more than `landed` with the scenery hidden: that's not the scenery. Building a terrain stalls the first frames (new shaders): hidden under the start screen on a new game.
