# The planet shot: a procedural planet and stars, zooming in, before cutscene 1

**Status:** approved 2026-11-09 (owner: "go", with the answers below). **Pass 1 built 2026-11-09**; passes 2 and 3 next, a stop after each.

> **[Agent note]** From the owner's `resources/models/props/planet.blend` (read without saving, 2026-11-09) and the owner's answers in chat the same day.

## Goal

A short shot played **before the intro cinematic** (cutscene 1, `0100`): the planet in space, the lens zooming from 20 mm to 180 mm very slowly, with two jump cuts closer so the shot stays short. On the last cut (about 150 → 180 mm) the planet turns a little. It's built as **its own page** (`planet.html`, like `synth.html`) and put into the game later.

The planet and the stars are **procedural**: generated by a shader while the game runs, tunable with sliders, saved as presets, so the same planet can be shown anywhere.

## Scope

**In:** the planet material, the atmosphere, the stars, the presets, the page with its panel, and the three-shot sequence with its timings editable on the page.

**Out (later):**
- **Post-processing**: the file's Bloom + Fog Glow, and the sun glint's glow (owner: later). Until then, the halo and glint look flatter than in Blender.
- **Putting it in the game** before cutscene 1 (NEW GAME → planet → cutscene 1), with skip.
- The baked fallback (the shader rendered into a cubemap): only if a scene needs it for speed.
- Sound.

## What's in the Blender file

- **Planet**: a smooth-shaded icosphere, 162 vertices, radius 1, tilted (0.41, −0.40, 0.17 rad). Owner: **it stays low poly**, as part of the look.
  - **Material** (`Planet - Mixed`): a 16-octave fBm height (scale 4.59, lacunarity 1.81), its W warped by a second 16-octave noise. A hard step at 0.5 splits land from sea; that step also sets roughness and the bump (0.2).
  - **Colours**: sea from a blue ramp. On land, a latitude band mixed with a third noise picks a green ramp or a white/cyan one. The colour goes to base colour and to emission (0.1), plus a white Fresnel rim (IOR 1.2).
- **Atmosphere**: the same mesh at 1.024, blue, alpha = Fresnel (1.07) + 0.2 × a cloud noise.
- **Stars** (the world, driven by view direction): two Voronoi dot layers (scale 80 and 200) tinted by a stepped ramp (yellow, red, blue, white), their brightness patchy from a large noise ×2, plus a very faint nebula noise.
- **Camera**: 8.54 m from the planet, 50 mm, 36 mm sensor, no depth of field. **Sun**: strength 7, white. ACES 1.3, which matches the game's ACES tone mapping.
- The softness and grain in the owner's 180 mm shot are EEVEE's (owner): not reproduced.

- Promote on second use (CLAUDE.md): the planet code stays in 0.0.2 (`src/planet/`).
- Gamepad first: the page is a dev tool like `synth.html` (mouse panel). The shot in the game will take the cutscene's skip, later.
- No conflicts found.

## Choices made

1. **Tuned in the game, with Three's noise** (option A from chat). The owner wants to pick a nice result, not Blender's exact continents. Blender's values are the starting point, but Three's noise differs from Blender's, so the result is re-tuned on the page. Rebuilding Blender's noise in TSL (option B) would let Blender stay the tuning tool: about one more pass, set aside.
2. **4D noise becomes 3D with a domain warp.** TSL has no 4D noise. Blender's W warp is copied as an offset added to the position by a second noise (the same effect). A **seed** moves the noise to a new place, so each seed gives a new planet.
3. **The detail follows screen size.** The number of octaves comes from the pixel's size on the surface: few when the planet is small, up to 16 at 180 mm. That keeps it cheap and stops fine octaves from shimmering.
4. **Stars stay the same size on screen, and as dense** (owner, 2026-11-09: "hold the density"; finer star layers fade in as the lens closes) (owner: "constant is better if it costs nothing"; it costs nothing). Each star's radius is measured in pixels, not in angle, so a 9× zoom doesn't make them 9× bigger. They're also smoothed over a pixel so they don't flicker.
5. **Blender's lens copied exactly:** the 36 mm sensor (Three's `filmGauge = 36`, then `setFocalLength`). At 20 mm the planet is about 15% of the frame's width; at 180 mm it overflows the frame, as in the owner's shot.
6. **The zoom is even to the eye:** the focal length grows exponentially within a shot (each second multiplies it by the same amount), so the zoom doesn't seem to speed up. Linear is a switch on the page.

## The work

### Pass 1: the planet and the page

- `src/planet/planet.ts`: `createPlanet(preset)` returns a group (planet + atmosphere) on the low-poly icosphere. It has `update(preset)` for the panel, and its own rotation (tilt and spin) so it can turn.
  - The material in TSL, as in Blender: warped fBm height → land/sea step → ramps, latitude band, bump, roughness, emission, Fresnel rim.
  - Ramps as a few colour stops (the panel edits each stop's colour and position).
- `src/planet/presets.json` + a dev save endpoint (the synth's, made general for both pages, not copied).
- `planet.html` + `src/planet/page.ts`: Blender's camera and sun, lil-gui with every value, *Save preset*, *Copy preset*, a seed slider, a focal-length slider (20–180 mm) to check any framing.

**Agent build:** about 45 min. **Review rounds:** 2–3 on the look (about 15 min each, mostly sliders).

> **As built (2026-11-09; actual: about 15 min, the session's 25 with the plan).** As planned: `src/planet/planet.ts`, `presets.json` (preset *Blender*), `page.ts`, `planet.html`; the synth's save endpoint made general (vite.config.ts `savedPresets`, both pages). Checked: type-check and build pass; headless screenshots at 20, 50 and 180 mm (1920×1080) look like the Blender render in character, no console errors; **about 4–5 ms a frame at 180 mm** (planet filling the screen; 0.4 ms at 20 mm).
> - **Found: Blender's coast bump draws stair-steps along every coast here** (screen derivatives work in 2×2 pixel blocks; Blender samples the height three times instead). The preset has it at 0 (Blender: 0.2); the slider stays. Doing it as Blender does would triple the planet's cost.
> - The material is Lambert (Blender's Principled has IOR 1: no specular on the planet). The glint in the Blender render comes from the atmosphere (pass 2).
> - The mesh is Three's icosahedron with the same 162 vertices and 320 faces as Blender's ico sphere, but not the same vertex layout. Exporting Blender's mesh is a small change if the outline should match exactly.
